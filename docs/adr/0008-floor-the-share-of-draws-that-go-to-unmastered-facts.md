# Floor the share of draws that go to Facts still to be mastered

ADR 0005 fixed selection weighting so that a few stubborn Facts could outweigh a crowd of fluent ones: weight is the Fact's Fluency as a multiple of its own target, squared, with a quarter-weight damper on Mastered Facts already practiced today. That took the worked example (a 5 × 5 range with 23 fluent Facts and 2 stubborn ones) from 73% of questions landing on already-fluent Facts down to 18%.

It still leaves the ratio at the mercy of headcount. The gap between "just inside the target" and "just outside it" is small, and squaring a small ratio keeps it small, so as a range approaches the 90% needed to expand, the Facts blocking progress can quietly fall back to a minority of the draws. That is precisely the moment the Learner most wants them: at 90% Mastered, only a handful of Facts stand between them and a new row and column, and a session that spends most of its questions re-confirming the other 90% feels like it isn't going anywhere.

**Selection now guarantees a floor on the share of draws that go to Facts which aren't Mastered yet.** That's `UNMASTERED_SHARE_FLOOR` in engine.ts, currently `0.5`. Those are exactly the Facts standing between the Learner and the next Active range expansion, or, at the top range where there is no expansion left to earn, between them and mastering the whole grid. The rule needs no special case for the top range: "not Mastered yet" already means the same thing at 12 × 12 as at 3 × 3.

It is implemented as a **group rescale, not a reserved pool**. `selectionWeights` computes every candidate's ordinary weight, and if the not-yet-Mastered ones hold less than the floor's share of the total, scales that whole group up until they hold exactly it. Solving `u' / (u' + m) = F` for the lifted unmastered total gives `u' = m · F / (1 − F)`, one multiplier applied across the group. Three things fall out of doing it this way rather than by drawing from a reserved pool:

- **Every other selection rule survives untouched.** Within the group, ADR 0005's ordering still decides which Fact comes up (the slowest is still the likeliest), and the boost multiplier, the decay term and the same-day damper all still apply. A reserved pool would have had to re-derive all of that.
- **Mastered Facts keep their long tail.** Their weight is scaled down *relative to* the group, never to zero. ADR 0005 deliberately refused to exclude fluent Facts, because at 90% Mastered that collapses the pool to a handful of Facts and drills them back to back; this doesn't reopen that decision.
- **A draw still costs one `random()`.** A reserved-pool design would have to roll once to choose the pool and again to choose within it, which would have quietly reshuffled every deterministic test in the suite for no behavioural gain.

It is a **floor, not a quota**: when the natural weighting already sends more than the floor's share to unmastered Facts (the normal state right after an expansion, when the new row and column are all unattempted and heavily weighted), the weights are returned untouched. Nothing is ever pulled *down* to the floor.

`UNMASTERED_SHARE_FLOOR` is a tuning knob, not a principle, and is now the first dial to turn when practice feels too generous to the easy Facts, ahead of ADR 0005's exponent, because it states the goal directly ("half my questions should be ones I still need") rather than steering it indirectly through the shape of a curve. `0` restores pure ADR 0005 weighting. Values approaching `1` are a bad idea for the reason above: they starve the Mastered Facts that the damper, not an exclusion, exists to keep alive.
